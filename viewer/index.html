Topology

Cells

A gate-level cell -- and, nand, mux, andnot, ... Labelled with its kind, not drawn as a shape per kind: there are thirteen of them and thirteen invented outlines would be harder to read than the word. The box is a container: what it holds is what it lowers into. nor -- realisable directly, so it holds the one torch it is. Nothing to lower. merge -- a wire merge, also realisable directly, and not a gate body at all: no torch, no support, just the point where several branches' dust are allowed to touch. A merge whose every branch is bare holds no blocks, and its spokes reach the dust it joins inside other cells. Primary input (a lever)  Primary output (a lamp) Signal flow, producer to consumer. Each edge lands on its own pin, stacked down the cell's left edge in the kind's declared pin order -- andnot and mux are not symmetric in their pins, so which edge lands where is part of what the circuit is.

Inside a cell

A merge among the cell's own lowered gates, with dashed spokes to the nodes whose dust joins there. A shared torch -- most expansions start by inverting an input, so !x is built once and reused. It is drawn inside the cell that built it, ringed in violet, with a dashed violet line to every other cell's expansion that reads it. Those cells therefore hold fewer primitives than their recipe has steps, and the missing one is on screen rather than implied. Zoomed out, a box's contents stop being legible and it shows ×n -- how many torches and merges it lowers into -- with +k for the shared torches it reads from elsewhere. Zoom in, or use Focus, to see them.
