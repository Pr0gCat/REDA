## Root cause and construction

These mixtures formerly fell through to the buffered general matrix. Its
entry, mismatch, and data stages delayed all three public drivers to tick 6.
The independent comparator prototype demonstrated shared primary supports
with private public-output rails, but the lossless adapter rejected two route
terminals naming the same physical support.

The generator now composes selected comparator inverter and subtraction
implementations. A primary support can feed both the product rear and a
negative-literal side, or two separate negative-literal sides. The internal
side inverter keeps its own handover support; its output is not joined to the
public negative output rail. Repeated conditions share the producer, with
distinct public lamps and distinct incoming terminal cells.

The new emission role identifies one shared primary comparator support by
its route and source. Every individual sink contract remains in the candidate
branches and undergoes the existing independent structural, ownership,
electrical interface and isolation verification. Adapter sharing requires
the same primary route, distinct consumers, connection targets, a solid
support, zero repeaters, no delayed owner, and one-cell root/terminal paths.
Generic terminals and declared-output landings remain nonshareable.
ExpandedStrict rule revisions become RouteContinuity 7, Connectivity 5,
Coupling 8; independently derived case identity snapshots track that policy.

## Validation and limits

Regression evidence includes the 18 shared-support combinations with all 32
retained-history phases, fail-closed adapter mutations, existing player tests,
independent structure and physical tests, adapter regressions, the revision
snapshot, 37 Native/fresh-Yosys source cases, constants, DFF and observed-output

This construction is still an explicit experimental player generator. It
does not enable default compiler selection, claim Minecraft runtime validation,
or finish the broader multi-condition/reconvergence objective. Mixtures with
constants, sums and additional product polarities remain subsequent work.
Block counts and volume do not determine acceptance.
